Reinventing Manufacturing: Stratified Workforce Empowerment with Embodied AI

Fu, X., Mathiassen, L., & Ramasubbu, N.

A U.S. auto-glass plant put embodied AI on its shop floor. Workers came out of it describing their jobs as more meaningful — and the ones who grew the most, taking on more control and more responsibility at once, were the least likely to be promoted. The firm's promotion categories had been built for a divided shop floor, and could not classify the people who had stopped fitting it.

When Better Opportunities Backfire

Fu, X., Luo, S., Ramasubbu, N., Shen, P., Wang, X., & Wang, Y.

An insurer handed its cold calls to an AI voice agent, leaving human agents a better set of leads to work. Their conversion on the day's first calls fell. Expecting something better next makes the thing in your hand easier to drop: early in the shift agents spent measurably less time on each promising lead, and were readier to push it back to the shared queue. The behavioural response swamped the mechanical gain.

−4.5ppfall in conversion on the first calls of the day
−13%less time spent on those early high-intent calls
567koutbound calls around a sharp AI routing expansion

The Privacy Asymmetry of Service Robots

Zhang, A., Fu, X., Maruping, L. M., & Liu, S.

When a robot rather than a person brings something to a hotel-room door, guests protect two kinds of privacy in opposite directions at once: they open the door wider and go quiet. Physical guard drops, informational guard goes up. Any service design that treats "privacy" as one dial will get one of them wrong.

−0.79spatial boundary protection — the door opens wider
+1.03data boundary protection — the guest says less

Sustaining Detection of Errors in LLM Output

Fu, X., Ramasubbu, N., & Galletta, D.

Being shown why an AI gets things wrong does almost nothing. Being made to explain it in your own words works — and then decays a little every day you keep using the tool. Oversight fails not because people don't know models err, but because that knowledge isn't there at the moment they're looking at the answer.

+7.5ppmore errors caught after explaining in your own words (N = 400)
−2.5ppof that advantage lost per day of routine use

Calibrating Epistemic Convergence in Generative AI

Rai, A., Fu, X., & Xia, Y.

Ask a room of people the same programming question and you get genuinely different answers. Ask a model the same question many times and the answers cluster — and newer models cluster tighter, not looser. Broadening the prompt, raising the temperature and keeping prior answers out of the context pull some of the variety back, but nowhere near all of it.

375Stack Overflow questions, sampled after every model's training cutoff
2.4–6.8%of the narrowing those three levers recover — the answers stay far more alike than human ones

When Transparency Backfires

Yang, J., Fu, X., Ramasubbu, N., & Fan, C.

Kickstarter made AI disclosure mandatory and funding fell across the platform. Projects that had built their pitch on human craft were shielded from the general suspicion and punished hardest when they actually disclosed. Authenticity works as armour right up until the moment it is contradicted.

12,521Kickstarter projects around the disclosure mandate
~1 in 10of the projects that could disclose actually did
